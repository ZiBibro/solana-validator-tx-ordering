Method
------

A handoff window is a window of 4 slots (first slot divisible by 4) whose leader differs from
the leader of the slot before it. Its residual is the node time of its first slot minus a
prediction from the last slot with an event before the window (the anchor), stepped forward
with the median slot-to-slot step at each position in the window over the 3000 slots before.
This mirrors handoff_residuals in probe/src/slots.rs, which steps with the probe's model.
5 089 handoff windows of 534 leaders; 5 085 of them had the anchor on the slot just before
the window.

Filter: of the 5 289 windows of 4 slots in the stream, 119 have no event on their first slot,
47 follow a slot of the same leader, 5 have no event in the 4 slots before, and 29 near the
start of the stream have fewer than 5 steps at some position in the 3000 slots before (the
steps are taken once per 100 slots); 5 089 remain.

  residual ms over 5 089 windows: median 0, p5 -35, p25 -15, p75 47, p95 133, p99 182,
  min -551, max 530

A leader's window starts at a steady offset: the spread inside a leader is small next to the
spread between leaders. That offset is what the correction learns.

The error falls fastest up to k = 3 (25.5 to 18.6 ms) and slowly after it. The harm falls
from 0.18 and 0.22 % at k 1 and 2 to 0.05 % at k 3. One outlying window decides a median of
1 or 2 windows and cannot move a median of 3. Hence 3.

Counted over every handoff window, a correction from the first earlier window on (minimum 1)
gives a lower mean error than minimum 3: 21.7 against 25.0 ms, share above 100 ms 0.033
against 0.055. The difference is coverage: minimum 3 leaves 688 more windows uncorrected,
those of leaders seen once or twice so far in the stream. The probe keeps the stream for the
whole run (KEEP_SLOTS 20 000 slots, about 2.2 hours), and in the dry run below the smallest
count at signing was 3. Minimum 3 gives up that coverage at the start of a stream for fewer
harmful corrections.


Bound
-----

250 ms lies above every leader median in the stream (largest 205 ms over 3 windows, largest
over 8 or more windows 158 ms) and below single windows, which reach -551 and 530 ms. A
median past 250 ms would come from a leader no window here showed, and the bound caps the
shift there. It is wider than 165 ms, as the decision asked. On this stream the bound changes
no number at minimum 3: mean 25.0 ms with a bound of 165, 200 or 250 ms and with none.


Replay on the 116 stored bursts
-------------------------------

As decision B4 asked: for each burst, c is the median of the prediction residuals of the same
leader's other bursts whose anchor was 1 to 4 slots before their window, those windows being
handoffs. c is 0 under 3 such bursts and is clamped to 250 ms. It applies only when the
burst's own anchor was before its window and its window is a handoff, as in the probe.
dev' = dev + c. The leader of the slot before each window comes from the
round*-leader-schedule.json snapshots of the runs; 1 of the 116 windows is not a handoff.
